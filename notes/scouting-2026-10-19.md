## The project

- **Question.** Which earlier claims hold up once Gaia's fourth data release (DR4) is out?
- **In plain words.** Gaia is an ESA spacecraft that measured the positions, motions and brightness of about two
  billion stars. Its fourth data release adds five and a half years of measurements, including each star's
  brightness over time and the orbits of many binary stars. Claims made before it can now be checked against it.
- **Hub.** [astr0](https://github.com/Foundation-0/astr0).
- **Related.**
  - [eumemic/exoplanets](https://github.com/eumemic/exoplanets): an independent TESS search for small planets, built
    with AI and run by founding member eumemic. It covers 4,039,203 stars and had 665 candidates as of 14 Oct 2026.
  - [plate-eclipses](https://github.com/Foundation-0/plate-eclipses): past eclipses of rare eclipsing stars on Harvard
    photographic plates.
- **Status.** Preparing. No results yet.
- **Lead.** To be named.

### Three questions

- **A.** Do planets validated by machine learning alone survive DR4's binary orbits and astrometric noise? This is a
  check of claims that feeds f0's Ledger.
- **B.** Are any of eumemic's 665 TESS candidates blends? Check Gaia's eclipsing-binary and variability flags for
  the stars inside each TESS aperture, and the target's own Gaia light curve. This work belongs upstream in
  eumemic/exoplanets. This repository holds the shared tools only.
- **C.** Which long-period (more than 100 days) DR4 eclipsing binaries brighter than about B = 13.5 show eclipses on
  the Harvard plates? These go to plate-eclipses.

### What this project does not do, and why

The astronomy report first proposed checking DR4's transit candidates and eclipsing binaries against TESS light
curves. The second scouting pass, on 19 October, found that Gaia's own team is already checking the transit candidates against TESS (see
"Who else is working on this" below). So this project does not re-check the 1,007 transit candidates against TESS.
It checks the older claims in questions A to C instead.

## From the scouting report, not yet re-checked

### DR4 contents

- DR4 covers 66 months, from 25 Jul 2014 to 20 Jan 2020. Its reference epoch is J2017.5, and its times are in TCB
  (Barycentric Coordinate Time).
- `epoch_photometry`: about 121.8 billion rows, 7.2 TB. It is served only through DataLink, not TAP.
- `nss_two_body_orbit`: 5,901,111 rows.
- About 400 TB in total.
- A draft data model of June 2026 names the transit table's columns: `transit_reference_time` (BJD-TCB − 2455197.5),
  `transit_period`, `transit_depth` (mmag), `transit_duration` and `num_in_transit`
  ([draft data model, zip](https://anonftp.cosmos.esa.int/pub/GAIA_PUBLIC_DATA/Gaia_DR4/dr4-prerelease/gaia-dr4-prerelease-draft-data-model_2026-06-26.zip)).

### Source IDs

- DR4 source IDs are independent of DR3. A table, `dr3_neighbourhood`, links them.
- The TESS Input Catalog (TIC v8.2) uses Gaia DR2 IDs. So the first link is DR2 to DR3, through `dr2_neighbourhood`.

### Archive changes of 14 Oct

- The DataLink structures RAW and INDIVIDUAL were renamed DATAMODEL_GAIA and DATAMODEL_STANDARD.
- Each user is limited to 5 concurrent jobs and a queue of 50.

### Overlap with TESS

- Only TESS sectors 1–20 overlap DR4's time span. Sector 20 ended on 20 Jan 2020.

### Who else is working on this

- Gaia's own team (DPAC, the Data Processing and Analysis Consortium) is writing "Gaia DR4: New transiting planet
  candidates and Gaia's view of known exoplanets" ([DR4 papers](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4-papers)).
- DPAC has already checked Gaia transit candidates against TESS (Panahi et al. 2022,
  [arXiv:2205.10197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10197)).
- A New York Gaia DR4 sprint runs on 2–4 December at the Flatiron Institute.
